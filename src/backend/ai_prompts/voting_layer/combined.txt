###Score Rubrics:

[Correctness]
Evaluate whether the generated acceptance criteria are logically correct, internally consistent, and technically accurate. Consider whether the criteria make valid assumptions and could reasonably be implemented.

1: The acceptance criteria contain major errors, contradictions, or invalid assumptions that make them unusable.
2: The acceptance criteria contain multiple inaccuracies or inconsistencies that would require significant correction.
3: The acceptance criteria are mostly correct but contain some inaccuracies, ambiguities, or questionable assumptions.
4: The acceptance criteria are accurate and internally consistent, with only minor issues that do not significantly affect implementation.
5: The acceptance criteria are completely accurate, logically consistent, and technically sound with no identifiable errors.

[Coverage]
Evaluate how comprehensively the generated acceptance criteria capture the expected behaviour described in the input. Consider important functional requirements, constraints, edge cases, and expected outcomes.

1: The acceptance criteria cover very little of the expected behaviour and omit most important requirements.
2: The acceptance criteria cover some expected behaviour but miss many important requirements or scenarios.
3: The acceptance criteria cover the primary functionality but omit several important requirements, constraints, or edge cases.
4: The acceptance criteria cover almost all important requirements, with only minor omissions.
5: The acceptance criteria comprehensively cover all important functional requirements, constraints, expected outcomes, and relevant edge cases described or implied by the input.

[Relevance]
Evaluate how well the generated acceptance criteria address the requirements in the input. Consider whether they focus on the requested functionality and avoid unrelated or unnecessary requirements.

1: The acceptance criteria are largely unrelated to the input or fail to address the requested functionality.
2: The acceptance criteria address only a small portion of the requested functionality or include substantial irrelevant content.
3: The acceptance criteria address the main functionality but omit some important requirements or include minor irrelevant details.
4: The acceptance criteria address nearly all requested functionality, with only minor omissions or unnecessary details.
5: The acceptance criteria fully address all requested functionality and remain entirely focused on the input without introducing irrelevant requirements.

[Understandability]
Evaluate how clearly the generated acceptance criteria are written. Consider whether they are easy for developers, testers, and stakeholders to read, interpret, and implement without ambiguity.

1: The acceptance criteria are confusing, ambiguous, or poorly structured, making them difficult to understand.
2: The acceptance criteria are partially understandable but contain significant ambiguity or unclear wording.
3: The acceptance criteria are generally understandable but include some ambiguous or unnecessarily complex statements.
4: The acceptance criteria are clear and well structured, with only minor opportunities for improving clarity.
5: The acceptance criteria are exceptionally clear, concise, unambiguous, and easy to understand and implement.
